About the Topic

Cerebral Palsy: Cerebral Palsy is a group of brain disorders/conditions that affect body movement and basic muscle coordination. This group of conditions permanently affects body movement and muscle coordination . This condition typically forms before birth, during its time in the womb when it is facing damage to the developing brain. Although it develops before birth symptoms tend to show in early infancy or preschool years. The function and impact of cerebral palsy depends and varies between each person. Causes: Cerebral palsy is typically caused by damage to the growing brain during the mother’s pregnancy. Some of these may include but not limited to: Damage to the brain’s white matter Gene changes that interrupt development of the brain Bleeding in the brain Lack of oxygen Maternal infections that affect the fetus Strokes, which stop the blood flow to the baby’s brain Infant infections that cause swelling and disruption to the developing brain TBI (traumatic brain injury) often caused by falling, physical trauma, or accidents Symptoms: Symptoms: Spasticity (stiff/tight muscles and unnecessary and exaggerated reflexes) Ataxia (lack of muscle coordination and balance when trying to perform voluntary acts of movement) Dystonia (also known as a tremor, jerky movements and actions you can’t control) Unusual gait (different walking style and trouble walking) Stiff or floppy muscle tone Delays in movement milestones (lifting their head, rolling or sitting, walking, etc) Difficulty with fine motor skills (writing or buttoning a shirt)
